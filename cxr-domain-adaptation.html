TL;DR: We compare multimodal alignment, adversarial domain adaptation, and supervised contrastive learning to transfer adult chest X-ray representations to pediatric pneumonia classification with limited labeled data.

Abstract

Adult and pediatric chest X-rays differ in anatomy and disease presentation, creating a domain shift for learned visual representations. This course project investigates whether pretraining on adult images can support pediatric pneumonia classification when labeled pediatric data is limited.

We compare a CLIP-style image–text dual encoder, a domain-adversarial neural network (DANN), and supervised contrastive learning (SupCon) against an ImageNet-initialized ResNet-50 trained on pediatric data. Our experiments examine F1 scores, ROC-AUC across training-set sizes, and t-SNE visualizations. In the reported settings, CLIP provides comparatively consistent transfer performance, while SupCon performs well on moderate-sized subsets and is sensitive to the full-data training setting.

Adult-to-pediatric transfer

The source domain is NIH ChestX-ray14. The target domain is the Kermany pediatric chest X-ray dataset, with normal and pneumonia labels. The transfer methods use a ResNet-50 image encoder with adult-domain SimCLR pretraining, followed by pediatric adaptation.

Source: adult chest X-rays

NIH ChestX-ray14: 112,120 images in the original dataset, with multiple pathology labels and a No Finding category.

Target: pediatric chest X-rays

Kermany dataset: 5,863 images in the original dataset, split for training, validation, and testing. Experiments vary the labeled pediatric training subset.

Baseline

An ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 with a binary classification head, trained on pediatric images using cross-entropy.

CLIP-style alignment

A ResNet-50 image tower and Bio_ClinicalBERT text tower map to a shared 512-dimensional space. Adult pretraining uses class-level prompts. Pediatric fine-tuning adapts the image encoder while freezing the text encoder.

DANN

A feature adapter and pneumonia classifier operate alongside an adult/pediatric domain discriminator. A scheduled gradient reversal layer encourages domain-invariant features while preserving the classification objective.

SupCon

Two augmented views per image support supervised contrastive learning. Same-class features are pulled together. Contrastive pretraining is followed by a combined binary classification and contrastive objective.

CLIP-style dual encoder

ROC-AUC rises in the larger-subset settings. The projected features show more distinct normal and pneumonia groups after fine-tuning.

Domain-adversarial neural network

DANN improves the reported F1 score but shows uneven ROC-AUC across data budgets. Suppression of useful domain-specific features is one possible explanation, rather than an experimentally established cause.

Supervised contrastive learning

SupCon reaches an F1 of 0.9238 in the 1,024-sample experiment. Its full-data setting has lower plotted ROC-AUC, motivating further study of class imbalance and sampling.

Discussion and next steps

These experiments illustrate tradeoffs between multimodal alignment, domain invariance, and class-aware feature separation. A stronger comparison would use identical data budgets and splits across methods, report variation across repeated runs, and isolate class-balancing choices.
