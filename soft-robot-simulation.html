Research overview

The project investigates computational building blocks for designing soft robotic tails. It combines heterogeneous material simulation and binary material optimization with residual physics learning and simplified hydrodynamic models.

These studies cover actuated beams, experimental trajectory matching, and a rigid-body/soft-tail simulation. Each provides a separate test of the modeling and optimization framework.

Differentiable physics · Soft robotics · Binary material optimization · DiffPD / C++ · Residual learning

Binary material optimization

I extended the DiffPD C++ core to support element-wise material assignments, including elastic energy, forces, and force differentials. A surrogate-gradient pipeline enables optimization over discrete soft/stiff configurations.

Actuated-beam experiments examine layouts under displacement and oscillation objectives. The study also compares DiffPD and Newton solvers across material stiffnesses and time-step sizes, and explores effective material descriptions and damping.

Residual physics: simulation-to-simulation

A residual force model corrects a baseline simulator toward a target simulator with different material parameters. I evaluated model inputs, loss modifications and curriculum learning over progressively longer rollouts.

Selected configuration: curriculum 0.1 s → 0.2 s → 0.5 s → 1 s; testing on 1 s; 64 substeps; chunk size 16; saved epoch 4. Original simulation: E = 215,856 Pa, ν = 0.45. Target simulation: E = 263,824 Pa, ν = 0.49.

Soft-tail hydrodynamics

I implemented Lighthill-inspired added-mass and resistive-force terms and examined how fluid effects alter tail oscillations. Frequency sweeps and displacement traces evaluate the model response.

Empirical drag/thrust coefficients were calibrated against a physics-based simulation baseline at 2, 7, 11, 15, and 18 Hz. Further experiments couple a deformable tail to rigid-body motion and evaluate cycle-averaged axial forces.

My contributions

  • Refined residual learning through curriculum design, model inputs, and loss changes.
  • Implemented fluid-force models and coefficient calibration experiments.
