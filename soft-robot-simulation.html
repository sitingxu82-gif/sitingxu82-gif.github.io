TL;DR: The project aims to optimize a robotic fish's physical behavior by configuring two soft materials within its tail. My work covers Neo-Hookean material simulation and optimization, residual learning, and simplified robotic fish modeling.

Research overview

The goal of this project is to simulate and experimentally study a robotic fish whose tail allows a freely configurable spatial distribution of two soft materials. By adjusting the material configuration, the project aims to optimize the fish's physical behavior.

My work focused on three parts of this effort:

  • Binary material simulation and optimization: simulated two Neo-Hookean soft materials with element-wise assignments and optimized their spatial configuration to achieve specified objectives.
  • Residual physics learning: conducted simulation-to-simulation and simulation-to-experiment studies with a residual model, toward using learned corrections to reduce the gap between simulated and experimentally observed behavior.
  • Robotic fish simulation: developed a simplified model connecting a rigid body to a deformable tail and incorporating hydrodynamic forces.

Binary material optimization

Binary material simulation and configuration optimization are implemented in the Newton framework, with each element assigned one of two Neo-Hookean soft materials. All simulations use SolverSemiImplicit. Surrogate gradients enable optimization of the discrete material configuration to achieve specified objectives.

The collaborating material laboratory conducted frequency-response experiments on beams with solid and liquid LMC configurations. Simulation-generated response curves are compared with the laboratory curves to assess the accuracy of the simulation and validate its representation of the the dynamic behavior of the beams.

Actuated-beam experiments evaluate material configurations under prescribed displacement and oscillation objectives. The work also investigates simulation stability, effective material descriptions, and damping within the semi-implicit simulation setup.

Uniform material configurations

Optimization objective

L=−1T∑t|xt−x¯|x¯=1T∑txt

xt is the mean x-position of the bottom nodes at time t, and x̄ is its temporal mean. Minimizing the negative mean absolute deviation maximizes oscillation amplitude around that mean.

Surrogate gradients and differentiable optimization

Each element has a configuration parameter pe. A hard threshold selects the liquid or solid material in the forward simulation. During backpropagation, a smooth surrogate replaces the derivative of this discrete switch, allowing the material configuration to be optimized through the simulated trajectory.

ze=Θ(pe−0.5)

ze = 0: liquid LMC · ze = 1: solid LMC

∂ze∂pe≈1(1+bs|pe−0.5|)2

Material-switch surrogate: bs = 0.5

The forward pass retains a binary material assignment. The surrogate provides an approximate optimization gradient rather than the exact derivative of the hard threshold.

Rayleigh damping

The simulation uses Rayleigh damping, combining mass-proportional and stiffness-proportional terms to represent damping in the actuated beam.

C=αM+βdKtanfdamping=−Cv

M is the mass matrix and Ktan is the tangent stiffness matrix. The damping coefficient βd is distinct from the surrogate steepness bs.

Mathematical details: material sensitivities and rollout gradients

Material-parameter sensitivities

For a material coefficient c, the binary selection can be written as:

c(pe)=cliquid+ze(csolid−cliquid)∂c∂pe≈csolid−cliquid(1+bs|pe−0.5|)2

This sensitivity propagates through the constitutive response and force assembly. The stiffness-proportional damping term also depends on the material configuration through Ktan.

Gradient through the semi-implicit rollout

Let sn = (qn, vn) and write a simulation step abstractly as sn+1 = Φ(sn, p). The configuration sensitivity obeys:

dsn+1dp=∂Φ∂sndsndp+∂Φ∂pdLdp=∑n∂L∂sndsndp+∂L∂p

The first term carries the influence of earlier steps; the second introduces the current step's material dependence. Backpropagation through the rollout applies this chain rule using the surrogate derivative at the material switch. The equations describe the sensitivity relationship without prescribing a particular autodiff implementation.

Residual physics: simulation-to-simulation

At each quadrature point, deformation features combine principal stretches, FᵀF − I, and det(F) − 1. Strain-rate, spin, and external-force features use the element's local rotation frame. The shared MLP outputs a three-dimensional correction; sample-mass scaling and force assembly produce the final nodal residual force.

A residual force model corrects a baseline simulator toward a target simulator with different material parameters. I evaluated model inputs, loss modifications and curriculum learning over progressively longer rollouts.

Soft-tail hydrodynamics

I implemented Lighthill-inspired added-mass and resistive-force terms and examined how fluid effects alter tail oscillations. Frequency sweeps and displacement traces evaluate the model response.

Empirical drag/thrust coefficients were calibrated against a physics-based simulation baseline at 2, 7, 11, 15, and 18 Hz. Further experiments couple a deformable tail to rigid-body motion and evaluate cycle-averaged axial forces.
