TL;DR: A physics-based engine model, automatic parameter calibration, and a sensor-connected software client bring measured engine behavior and real-time predictions into a digital twin workflow.

Project overview

Engine digital twins require models that are accurate enough to represent measured behavior and fast enough to support real-time monitoring. This project combines a zero-dimensional combustion model for an ammonia–diesel dual-fuel engine with automatic calibration and a Qt client connected to the experimental hardware.

The model predicts the in-cylinder pressure curve over crankshaft angle from engine and operating parameters. Historical operational measurements drive a multi-objective calibration procedure, updating model coefficients. The software integrates data acquisition, pressure prediction, and monitoring.

Real-time combustion model

The zero-dimensional model treats the cylinder working fluid as ideal and assigns computational points to distinct engine-cycle stages. Intake and exhaust flows use a quasi-steady approximation. Compression, expansion, and ignition delay use simplified thermodynamic descriptions to reduce computation.

A Hiroyasu–Arai spray model and empirical ammonia entrainment model describe fuel mixing. Arrhenius-based combustion rates represent premixed combustion, diffusion combustion, and afterburning for the two fuels.

Automatic calibration

The calibration procedure takes historical operational data as input and optimizes 33 coefficients. Sensitivity analysis identifies compression and combustion coefficients as major influences on model fidelity.

NSGA-II balances six evaluation objectives based on CA10, CA50, CA90, the peak-pressure point, air inflow, and peak-pressure mean squared error. These objectives connect model fitting to measured pressure and combustion timing.

Experimental validation

The manuscript validates model predictions against SJTU-SCE175 measurements under 13 operating conditions. The mean peak-pressure error is 3.92%. Mean CA10 and CA50 errors are 2.3° and 1.9°, respectively. Runtime remains below 2 milliseconds in every tested condition.

Software and system integration

The Qt client connects sensor measurements to an industrial control computer, supports data monitoring, and displays model-predicted pressure curves. Port communication and transmission protocols connect the hardware and software components.
