A target-conditioned locomotion task that combines commanded foot positions and yaw angles with alternating swing/support roles, enabling randomized forward stepping and continuous turning.

Simulation demonstrations

The videos demonstrate behavior in simulation. The task tracks discrete foot-placement targets rather than prescribing a full swing trajectory.

Target-conditioned stepping

The environment extends HumanoidVerse's legged-robot task for the Unitree G1 in Isaac Sim. Each swing-foot command specifies a world-frame 3D position and yaw angle. The opposite foot is assigned a support target, so learning must balance placement accuracy with support-foot stability.

After reset, the initial heading is synchronized with the robot's actual pose, and standing targets are set to the current foot positions. A configurable standing period precedes the first step. The first swing foot is randomized to reduce a fixed left/right ordering bias.

Event-triggered foot switching

A step is ready to switch when both position and yaw errors remain within their respective thresholds for a configurable hold time. A maximum swing duration provides a timeout fallback. At each switch, the previous swing target becomes the new support target, the swing-foot identity alternates, and a new target is sampled.

ep=p*−pswingeψ=wrap(ψ*−ψswing)

Yaw errors are wrapped to [−π, π]. Ground contact and body angular velocity are monitored, while the active readiness condition is target holding.

Two target-generation modes

Random forward stepping

Targets are sampled relative to the support foot. Forward displacement varies around the initial heading, while a signed lateral offset maintains left/right step width. Height and yaw commands are also randomized.

Curriculum difficulty expands the forward range, lateral range, directional spread, height range, and yaw variation. The reference heading remains the heading recorded at reset.

Circular turning

Targets use a fixed forward step length and signed lateral step width in the desired-heading frame. Each sampling event advances that heading by a fixed turn angle, including timeout-triggered switches.

The generator defaults are a 0.15 m step length, 0.14 m step width, and −8° heading increment. These parameters are configurable; successive steps form a polygonal approximation to a circle.

Learning framework

The task is integrated with a PPO-based training pipeline. Heading-frame commands and tracking errors express foot-placement objectives relative to the robot, while explicit swing-foot identity distinguishes the two roles.

Reward design

Separate left/right swing rewards track 3D position and yaw and provide a target-reach bonus. Support rewards encourage contact, XY position retention, and yaw tracking. Additional terms track nominal base height, discourage body tilt and upper-body deviation, and reward low velocity during the initial standing phase.

rp=exp(−‖ep‖2σp),rψ=exp(−eψ2σψ)

Roll and pitch angular velocity are penalized while yaw rotation remains available for turning. Upper-body action offsets are suppressed, and support-leg action offsets are scaled to 20% to reduce unwanted support-foot motion.

Adaptive curriculum

During training resets, difficulty increases when average episode length exceeds a configured fraction of the maximum episode length and decreases below a lower threshold. This gradually adjusts random-target ranges as the robot sustains longer episodes.

Monitoring distinguishes target-ready switches from timeout switches through a readiness ratio. A separate switch counter increments for both cases, so it is not used as a successful-placement metric.

My contributions
